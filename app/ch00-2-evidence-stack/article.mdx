## Six Evidence Languages, One per Part

  <div className="not-prose">

</Figure>

A short pass through each:

- **Description (Part I).** Reading data as business evidence — what is a dataset, what is the unit of observation, what business claim does a given table support? The work happens *before* visualization, regression, or prediction. Without it, every later step inherits its mistakes.
- **Visual evidence (Part II).** Visualization is its own evidence language, not a styling layer on top of analysis. Every later Part begins with the visual version of its question. The interval plot, the event-study chart, the calibration plot, the embedding scatter — Part II teaches them all *before* any method that needs them.
- **Causal designs (Part III).** Experiments and quasi-experiments that produce credible counterfactuals. The Part where "we observed it" becomes "we caused it."
- **Prediction (Part IV).** Supervised learning and segmentation on structured data. The Part where "what is likely?" gets a model with an evaluation rubric and a deployment plan.
- **AI workflows (Part V).** Embeddings, GPT-as-measurement, retrieval-augmented generation, vision, multimodal AI, agents. The Part where text, images, and language models become part of the workflow.
- **The system view (Part VI).** Running the analytics organization as a learning system rather than a sequence of projects. The Part where the methods become an operating discipline.

The order is deliberate: each Part adds an evidence language to the ones that came before. By Part V, a customer scored by a Part IV model is also a paragraph in a Part V complaint cluster. By Part VI, both feed one memo.

## The Artefact Family

The book is structured around five one-page artefacts that survive the work. They form a chain — the discipline of each one extends the one above it.

<Figure width="page-outset" caption="Figure 2. The five artefacts every Part of the book teaches you to write. The Decision Question Card is the parent; the Decision Memo is the synthesis. Each card carries a different question, but they share the same operating discipline.">
  <div className="not-prose">
    <ArtefactFamilyTree />
  </div>
</Figure>

Where to find each one in the book:

- **Decision Question Card (§9.1).** The parent artefact. Six lines: action, outcome, unit, timing, comparison, threshold.
- **Predictive Task Contract (§14.2).** The four-decision setup for any supervised model: target, features, unit of prediction, label timing.
- **Model Card (§15.5).** What a deployed model does, where it fails, who owns it.
- **AI Workflow Card (§22.1).** What an LLM-driven workflow does, what governs it, who responds when it breaks.
- **Decision Memo (§24.1).** The synthesis. The one-page document that ties every form of evidence into a recommendation an executive can sign.

A working analytics organization produces all five, every time. A research artefact stops at one or two. The chain is one way to tell the difference.

## How the Map Connects

The decision ladder, the evidence languages, the artefacts, the studios, the cases — these are not five separate organizational schemes. They are five views of the same structure:

- **The ladder** is the question hierarchy.
- **The evidence languages** are the answers at each rung.
- **The artefacts** are the durable outputs that survive the work.
- **The studios** are the integrated capstones at the end of each Part.
- **The cases** are the data the methods get tested against.

Hold all five in mind when you read the book. The next time you face an actual business decision, the right next move is usually to walk down the ladder until you find the rung that matches it — and then read the corresponding Part.

## A Brief Foreshadowing of Part VI

The book climbs the decision ladder and then turns to a different kind of question: not *what method should we use*, but *how do we run all of these methods together as one operating system*? That is Part VI's job. It introduces:

- **The Data Product View (§23.1).** Every artefact has a name, an owner, a version, a contract, and a refresh cadence.
- **The Decision Memo (§24.1).** The synthesis document; the page an executive signs.
- **Portfolio Monitoring and Learning Loops (§24.2).** How to run §17.4 and §22.2 side by side as one customer-analytics system.
- **The Final Integrative Case (§25.1).** A Bean &amp; Basket strategic decision that uses every Part's contribution at once.

We end where we started — naming the action, the counterfactual, and the threshold — but now with the full toolkit and the full operating model.
